Privacy Policy

Effective 8 October 2026

Mot Juste is made by Chris Mahlke. This policy explains what Mot Juste does with information on your iPhone and iPad.

The short version: Mot Juste has no accounts, no ads, no analytics, and no tracking, and I never receive any information about you. The dictionary is on your device, and so is the Apple Intelligence model that checks your sentences and writes your stories. If you use iCloud, your words sync through your own private iCloud storage, which I can't see.

What Mot Juste uses, and where it stays

Your words and lists. The words you save or edit, your lists, your favorites, and your place in the app are stored on your device. When you save a word you found somewhere, the sentence you found it in is saved with it.

Your progress. Your reviews, your Word Quiz history, and your grammar progress (the lessons you've read, your best scores, and your last answers) are stored on your device.

Your writing. Your Commonplace Book (the sentences you met words in, with the page, book, or photo they came from), the sentences you write with words in Use It, and the pieces you write for the weekly prompt are stored on your device. Writing Habits counts the words you lean on in the writing you check; only the counts are kept, on your device, and you can clear them. What you write about yourself, such as which kinds of writing you do, is stored on your device only.

Files you import. When you import a Kindle vocabulary file, a Kindle clippings file, or a file of highlights, Mot Juste reads it on your device and keeps only the words and sentences you choose.

Lists and challenges you share. When you share a word list or a writing challenge, Mot Juste makes a file with the list's words, or the prompt and the pieces written for it, and the name you sign with, if any. It goes only where you send it.

The Mot Juste keyboard. The optional keyboard works without Full Access. It suggests words from a list built into it; nothing you type is stored or leaves the keyboard.

Text you bring in. When you paste text, choose a photo, or scan a page, Mot Juste reads the words on your device so you can tap them. The camera is used only while the scanner is open, and nothing is recorded or stored. Photos are chosen with the system photo picker, so Mot Juste sees only the photo you pick and never asks for access to your library.

Words shared from other apps. When you share a word to Mot Juste from Safari, Books, or another app, the share extension passes the word, its sentence, and (from Safari) the page's title and address to Mot Juste on your device. The word is saved with its sentence.

Apple Intelligence. Use It, Stories, and Find the Word's suggestions use Apple's on-device model. What you write, and the words you ask about, never leave your device.

Today's words. The daily notification is scheduled on your device, with no server involved. The widgets read today's words from a file Mot Juste keeps on your device.

Spotlight, Siri, and the Mac's Services menu. Mot Juste adds its words to Spotlight on your device so you can find them in search, and answers "Define a word", "Find a word", "Compare two words", and "Sharpen text" through Siri and Shortcuts on your device. On a Mac, its Services read the text you select and put the result in its place, on the Mac.

Settings. Your appearance, your notification time, your recent searches, and whether you've seen the app's hints are stored on your device only.

Diagnostics. Mot Juste keeps a short log of errors and content changes on your device to help it recover from problems. It is never sent anywhere.

Information that leaves your device

iCloud. If you're signed in to iCloud, Mot Juste keeps a copy of the words you add or edit, your lists, your place in Words, your study progress, and your writing (your Commonplace Book, your sentences and pieces, and the close words you've told apart) in your private iCloud database, so they match on your iPhone, iPad, and Mac. Apple stores that copy under your Apple Account, under Apple's iCloud terms and privacy policy. I can't see it. Your quiz history, grammar progress, and writing habits aren't synced; they stay on the device where you made them.

Language data. To tell a word's part of speech in a sentence, Mot Juste asks iOS for its English language data, which iOS downloads from Apple the first time it's needed. That request comes from iOS, and nothing about you is sent to me.

Sharing and backups. When you share a word or a story, the image and its caption go only where you choose to send them. When you export a backup, the file is saved only where you choose.

Crash reports. If you choose to share analytics with app developers in the Settings app (Privacy & Security, Analytics & Improvements), Apple may send me anonymous crash reports. You can turn this off at any time.

Nothing else leaves your device. Mot Juste makes no network requests of its own, contains no third-party analytics, advertising, or tracking code, and does not share or sell any information.

Children

Mot Juste is suitable for all ages and collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Deleting your information

Everything Mot Juste stores is on your device, and in your own iCloud account if you use iCloud. Deleting the app deletes everything on the device. You can take any word out of My Words at any time, and manage what Mot Juste keeps in iCloud from the Settings app.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
